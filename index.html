All essential SAT grammar rules

IC = independent clause · DC = dependent clause · S = subject · V = verb

Read once. Apply repeatedly.

1. Sentence Boundaries

  • A complete sentence has S + V + a complete thought.
  • Never join two ICs with only a comma.
  • IC. IC.IC; IC.IC, FANBOYS IC.
The first trial failed; the second produced stable results.

2. Commas

  • After an opening dependent clause: DC, IC.
  • Before FANBOYS only when both sides are complete sentences.
  • Use paired commas around nonessential information.
  • Never split a subject from its verb.

3. Semicolons, Colons, and Dashes

  • Semicolon: joins two related ICs.
  • Colon: follows an IC and introduces a list or explanation.
  • Dash: adds an emphatic explanation; two dashes can enclose an interruption.

4. Coordination and Subordination

  • FANBOYS joins equal ideas; use a comma only for two ICs.
  • Although, because, if, since, unless, when, while make a clause dependent.
  • Choose a conjunction by logic: contrast, cause, condition, or time.

5. Clauses and Appositives

  • Restrictive: needed to identify the noun; no commas.
  • Nonrestrictive: extra detail; use paired commas or dashes.
  • An appositive renames a noun and needs matching punctuation when nonessential.

6. Subject-Verb Agreement

  • Find the true subject; ignore interrupting phrases.
  • In inverted order, the subject may follow the verb.
  • With neither...nor, agree with the nearer subject.
  • Each, either, neither, everyone are singular.

7. Verb Tense, Form, and Mood

  • Keep the timeline consistent.
  • Use past perfect for the earlier of two past events.
  • Use correct participles: has written, not has wrote.
  • After recommend that, use the base form: be, submit, revise.

8. Pronouns and Possessives

  • Pronouns must agree clearly with their antecedents.
  • Use subject case for doers and object case after verbs or prepositions.
  • Its is possessive; it's means it is/has.
  • Whose is possessive; ordinary plurals take no apostrophe.

9. Modifiers

  • Place a modifier beside the word it describes.
  • An opening phrase must logically describe the subject immediately after the comma.
  • Ask: Who is doing the action in the opening phrase?
Walking through the gallery, Leo admired the paintings.

10. Parallelism and Comparisons

  • Match forms in lists and paired structures.
  • Keep both X and Y and not only X but also Y balanced.
  • Compare like with like: a city's climate with that of another city.

11. Five-Second Boundary Check

12. Fast Decision Strategy

  1. Bracket the clauses.
  2. Find the core S-V pair.
  3. Map the timeline.
  4. Check the logical doer.
  5. Test parallel forms.
  6. Eliminate rule violations.
